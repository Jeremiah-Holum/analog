# THE BRENNER FILE: series bible

The series is a set of recovered recordings about one office building in Coulee County, Wisconsin,
the third floor inside it, and everywhere that floor went after the building was gone.

This file is **for the writer, not the audience.** The videos never explain any of this outright.
They leave clues, and viewers piece it together. Anything marked *(writer's eyes only)* is never
stated on screen; it's here so every video stays consistent.

**Released so far:** ITEM 14 *(THE THIRD FLOOR)*. **In production:** ITEM 15 *(SPACE AVAILABLE)*.

## 1. The short version

In 1968 a night custodian counted the offices on the third floor of the new Brenner Mutual
Insurance building. There were nine. He counted ten. He knocked on the tenth.

For the next thirty years, anyone who counted past nine on that floor found the hallway longer
than it should be, and a door at the end with no number. Some went through and never came back.
The ones who did what the memo said (stopped counting, walked back to the elevator, didn't run)
went home safe. **They weren't.** Their count was still open, and it followed them. Years later,
in their own houses, a hallway would have one more door than it should.

In 1998 the building was demolished, and its **doors were sold for salvage.** They went into
farmhouses, a motel, a church rectory. Every building with one of those doors in it now has a
third floor, whether it has three floors or not.

A county detective spent seven years collecting every recording connected to it. **ITEM 14**,
Dale Kessler's tapes, was the first to leak.
Someone is now posting the rest as **VHS-C 14**.

*(writer's eyes only)* Watching a count is counting. The tapes spread it too. The uploader knows.

## 2. How it works

### The public rules (Facilities Notice 71-117, R. Pruitt, March 12, 1971)
1. The third floor contains nine (9) offices.
2. If you count more than nine, do not continue. Return to the elevator. Do not run.
3. Do not knock on any door that is not numbered.
4. If something knocks, do not answer.
5. Do not look at the end of the hall for longer than necessary.

Pruitt's rules are right about the building. They're wrong about everything after.

### The truth under the rules *(writer's eyes only)*

**Counting is the invitation.** Counting doors is how the floor measures you. Up to nine, you're
counting the building. Past nine, the building is counting you.

**An unclaimed door has no number.** The door at the end of the hall is blank until someone is
going to be behind it. As you keep counting, doors take numbers: 310, 311, 312...

**The hall always ends at your door.** However far you count is the number of your office. Dale
counted fourteen on November 7th, 1994. His office is **314**. It was already there.

**Knocking is asking.** Three slow knocks mean *"are you coming in?"* Any answer is yes, even
"hello?" or "who's there?" Dale answered on the very first night.

**The Occupant only moves between looks.** Staring at it feels safe, and it is, but only for as
long as you stare. Rule 5 exists because nobody can stare forever, and while you're looking at
the end of the hall you aren't watching anything else.

**Unfinished counts travel.** If you count past nine and then stop (rule 2), the count stays
open. It goes home with you. It's patient; it takes years. Eventually a hallway where you live
(your own upstairs, your apartment building, your mother's house) has one more door than it
should. Usually it's in a place you walk past every day without counting. The first time you
*do* count, you finish.

**Unfinished counters are found facing a wall.** When an open count finishes somewhere that isn't
the third floor, the person is found standing with their face to a blank wall, where the door
was. Fingernails full of drywall. No cause of death.

**The doors carry it.** After 1998, the building's salvaged doors carry the floor with them. Hang
one in any building and that building has a third floor. Count the rooms of the house and the
salvaged door is always the tenth, even if the house only has four rooms.

**0141 still rings.** The third-floor phone extension, 555-0141, was disconnected in March 1971.
It still calls people at **2:11 AM**, usually counters, in the voice of someone the floor has kept.
Picking up is fine. **Answering a question** ("how many offices did you count?") is how the count
finishes over the phone.

**The tapes carry it.** Counting along with a recording is counting. Det. Frank Weller learned this
in 1997, the hard way. Amundsen sealed the file in 1999 because of it.

**The floor is a tenant building.** Every door past nine is an office, and every office has a
tenant. They aren't dead exactly. From outside you sometimes hear them faintly: typing, counting,
knocking, a phone ringing. The floor uses their voices. It advertises. It wants to be full.

**When 317 is let, the floor is full.** Gary counts to seventeen at 2:11 AM in ITEM 15: the
doors that are coming. 315 is Frank Weller (1997). 316 is Rick Dunaway (1998). **317 has no
name.** *(finale)* When the floor is full it doesn't stop. It builds another floor.

## 3. The Occupant

- About **3.2 m (10'5")** tall, too tall for any building it's in. It stoops under the ceiling
  tiles, head hanging forward, one shoulder lower than the other. Its arms reach past its knees,
  and its fingers are long and thin.
- **No face.** It's never shown clearly, ever. Production rule: backlit, silhouetted, at the edge
  of frame, or only its legs and hands.
- It moves **only between looks**: a flicker, a camera turn, a tape cut, a blink.
- It never uses its own voice. It **borrows tenants' voices**: Pruitt's on the phone in 1994,
  Gary's on the 1996 tape, Dale's on his wife's answering machine.
- It appears wherever the floor is: the third floor, the salvaged doors, the end of any hallway
  where an open count is finishing.
- *(writer's eyes only)* It isn't one person, and it isn't exactly a monster. It's **the shape
  the floor makes when it wants to be a person.** It stoops because no building was ever built
  big enough for what the third floor holds. Every tenant makes it a little taller. It was 7'1"
  in 1968; by 1998 it doesn't fit in a doorway.

## 4. The place, and the places after it

**Brenner Mutual Insurance Building**, 1100 Fourth Street, Coulee County, Wisconsin
- Four floors, about 40,000 sq ft. Designed by Leonard Asch, built 1967–68.
- Every floor was designed with nine private offices. The architect's plans, the city's floor
  plans and every fire inspection agree: **nine**.
- The third floor was closed in March 1971 and never reopened.
- Brenner Mutual left suddenly in November 1994. The building stood empty, **received no offers**,
  and was demolished in May 1998. It's a parking lot now. The attendant's booth is where the
  elevator shaft was.

**Where the doors went** (Halloran Architectural Salvage, Lot 7: *"15 interior doors, oak veneer,
brass hardware, one without plaque"*)

| Door | Went to | What happened there |
|---|---|---|
| 301–304 | A church, St. Olaf's rectory (four doors, upstairs hall) | Father Kaczmarek, 2001 |
| 305, 306 | Two houses in Onalaska, as closet doors | Unknown. Never traced. |
| 307 | Coulee After Dark studio (public access), as a set piece | Burned with the studio, 1999. Or didn't. |
| 308 | Halloran's own office | Bud Halloran, 2002 |
| 309 | Lakeview Motel, as the door of Room 10 | The motel, 2003 |
| 310–314 | A storage unit, rented by someone paying cash | Opened in 2019. Empty. |
| **The blank door** | **The Engstrom farmhouse near Holmen, as a closet door** | **Nora, 2000** |

**Leonard Asch's other buildings** *(season 3 seed)*: Asch designed eleven more buildings in
the region between 1968 and 1979. Every one of them has **nine offices per floor**, even where
that makes no sense (a clinic, a school, a parking ramp). Nobody knows why. His original drawing
for Brenner Mutual, on tracing paper, shows **ten** offices on three. The tenth is erased.

## 8. Seasons

**Season 1: THE BUILDING (1966–1994).** The third floor and the people who worked around it. ITEMS
1–14, plus 16, 17 and 18. The mystery: *what is up there?* Keystone episodes: **14** *(released)*,
**4** (Harold's reels, audio only over photos of the brand-new empty building), **9** (Pruitt's
dictation), **17** (Pruitt's answering machine).

**Season 2: THE SALVAGE (1995–2003).** It follows people home. The building is gone, but the
floor isn't. ITEMS 15, 19–30, 33. The mystery: *where is it now?* Keystone episodes: **15**
*(in production)*, **20** (the live broadcast), **23** (the emergency broadcast, where the series
widens from one building to *any* building), **25** (Nora's farmhouse, the first child).

**Season 3: THE FILE (2004–now).** The detective, the daughter and the uploader. ITEMS 31 and 32,
the unlabeled tape, and Asch's other buildings. The mystery: *who is sending these, and why?* The
finale answers that, not what the floor is.

## 9. Motifs and easter eggs (keep these consistent)

- **9**: the official count. Every document says nine.
- **2:11 AM**: Harold's hour. Put it on a clock, a timestamp or a receipt in every video.
- **The desk clock stopped at 2:00.**
- **Three knocks**, slow and heavy.
- **555-0141**: the dead third-floor line. It shows up on signs, business cards and phone bills.
- **CAM 05**: a feed that shouldn't exist, the Occupant's point of view.
- **Facing the wall**: how unfinished counters are found.
- **Wrong date stamps**: on the third floor, clocks drift to the floor's night. (Dale's last tape
  is stamped the morning *before* he arrived for that shift.)
- **Door plaques 310–317.** 317 is never shown with a name.
- **"Room to grow." "Priced to move." "Space available."** The building is always cheerfully for rent.
- **Oak veneer, brass hardware.** Once the doors are salvaged, a nice old oak door is the scariest
  object in any shot.
- **Flicker, then movement.** The Occupant is never seen moving, only having moved.
- **Counting on screen.** Every video has one moment that invites the viewer to count along.
  *(writer's eyes only: that's the point.)*

## 10. Mysteries that stay mysteries

- What the Occupant is.
- Whether the tenants are alive, and whether they can be let go.
- Why Asch erased the tenth office, and why all his later buildings have nine.
- What happened to Denny Szabo on the night of the broadcast.
- Where doors 305, 306 and 310–314 are now.
- Whether Amy Kessler is right.
- Who is behind door 317.
