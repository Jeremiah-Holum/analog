# THE BRENNER FILE: series bible

The series is a set of recovered recordings about one office building in Coulee County, Wisconsin,
and the third floor inside it.

This file is **for the writer, not the audience.** The videos never explain any of this outright.
They leave clues, and viewers piece it together. Anything marked *(writer's eyes only)* should
never be stated on screen; it's here so every video stays consistent.

---

## The short version

In 1968 a night custodian counted the offices on the third floor of the new Brenner Mutual
Insurance building. There were nine. He counted ten.

Since then, anyone who counts past nine finds the hallway longer than it should be and a door at
the end that isn't on the floor plan. Behind it is an office that's waiting for them. Something very
tall lives in the hall. It only moves when you're not looking at it.

A county detective spent seven years collecting every recording connected to the building and
numbered them in the order they were made. **ITEM 14** (Dale Kessler's tapes) was the first to be
leaked. The channel **VHS-C 14** is named after it and releases the rest out of order.

---

## The place

**Brenner Mutual Insurance Building**, 1100 Fourth Street, Coulee County, Wisconsin.
- Four floors, about 40,000 sq ft. Built 1967–68 for Walter Brenner's insurance company.
- Every floor was designed with **nine private offices**. The architect's plans, the city's floor
  plans and the fire inspection all agree: **nine**.
- The third floor was closed to staff in **March 1971** (Facilities Notice 71-117) and never
  reopened. Brenner Mutual kept working on floors 1, 2 and 4 for another 23 years.
- Brenner Mutual left **suddenly in November 1994**, the week after Dale Kessler disappeared.
- The building sat empty from 1994 to 1998, was listed for lease and sale (ITEM 15), received
  **no offers**, and was **demolished in 1998**. The lot is a parking lot now.

## The third floor: how it works

### The public rules (Facilities Notice 71-117, R. Pruitt, 1971)
1. The third floor contains nine (9) offices.
2. If you count more than nine, do not continue. Return to the elevator. Do not run.
3. Do not knock on any door that is not numbered.
4. If something knocks, do not answer.
5. Do not look at the end of the hall for longer than necessary.

### What's actually going on *(writer's eyes only)*
- **Counting is the invitation.** Counting the doors is how you let the floor measure you. Past
  nine, the hall starts adding length, and the count is now counting *you*.
- **An unclaimed door has no number.** The door at the end of the hall is blank until someone is
  going to be behind it. As you keep counting, the doors take numbers: 310, 311, 312...
- **The hall always ends at your door.** However many doors you count is the number of your
  office. Dale counted to fourteen on November 7th. His office is **314**. It was already waiting.
- **Knocking is asking.** Three knocks from inside a door means *"are you coming in?"* Answering,
  even saying "hello?", is saying yes. Dale answered on November 3rd (ITEM 14-A).
- **Looking too long gives it time.** The Occupant moves only when nobody is watching it. Staring
  at the end of the hall feels safe, but it means you aren't watching anything else: the doors
  behind you, the elevator, the camera feeds.
- **The offices beyond nine are occupied.** Each belongs to someone the floor has kept. They
  aren't dead exactly. They're *tenants*. From outside you sometimes hear them faintly:
  counting, knocking, typing, a phone ringing. The Gary tape ends with a tenant still counting at
  2:11 AM.

### The office behind the end door
Behind the blank door is an office that looks like any open-plan floor in the building: desks,
partitions, one lamp on. There is always a **TV** showing **CAM 05**. The building's security
system only ever had four cameras (CAM 01–04). **CAM 05 is the Occupant's point of view.** Whoever
is watching that TV is watching themselves from behind.

## The Occupant (the figure)

- About **3.2 m (10'5")** tall, too tall for the building. It stoops under the ceiling tiles, head
  hanging forward, one shoulder lower than the other. Its arms reach past its knees, and its
  fingers are long and thin.
- **No face.** It must never be shown clearly, ever. (Production rule: backlit, silhouetted, at
  the edge of frame, or only its legs and hands.)
- It moves **only between looks**: when the light flickers, when the camera turns, when the tape
  cuts.
- It never speaks with its own voice. It **borrows voices**: Pruitt's on the phone in 1994,
  Gary's on the 1996 tape. Anyone who has become a tenant is a voice it can use.
- *(writer's eyes only)* It isn't one person, and it isn't exactly a monster. It's **the shape the
  floor makes when it wants to be a person.** It stoops because the building was never built big
  enough for what the third floor actually holds. Every tenant it keeps makes it a little taller.

## The people

**Harold Voss**, night custodian, 1968. *Door 310.* The first counter. On **December 3, 1968, at
2:11 AM** he mopped the third floor, counted ten doors, and knocked on the tenth. He was never
found. **2:11 AM** has been the floor's hour ever since.

**Evelyn Marsh**, Brenner Mutual stenographer. *Door 311.* Worked late on three in the fall of
1970, typing up claims files. Her last pages are a claims form filled in with the numbers 1 to
311, over and over.

**Gary Lindqvist**, Brenner Mutual's leasing agent, 1971. *Door 312.* Hired in early 1971 to
lease out unused third-floor offices. He walked the floor with a prospective tenant, counted twelve
offices, and was gone by March. **Coulee Commercial Realty didn't exist until 1980**, so of course
they have no record of him. His extension, **555-0141**, was the third floor's line. It was
disconnected when the floor closed in March 1971. *(writer's eyes only)* The 1996 "walkthrough"
(ITEM 15) was not filmed by anyone living. The building made a sales tape of itself, in Gary's
voice, and left it in a realtor's drawer. The floor wants tenants. *Space available.*

**Robert Pruitt**, facilities manager, 1966–1979. *Door 313.* The one who figured out the rules.
After Evelyn and Gary he closed the floor, wrote Notice 71-117, and spent eight years making sure
nobody went up. He retired in 1979 and died at home in **1983**. His wife found him in their
upstairs hallway, standing with his face to the wall. *(writer's eyes only)* He'd started
counting the doors in his own house. When Dale called "Pruitt" in 1994, something answered with
his voice and told Dale not to come back up, the last kind thing Pruitt ever said, reused.

**Dale Kessler**, night security, 41. *Door 314.* Hired by Brenner Mutual in 1988. Recorded four
VHS-C tapes between November 3 and 9, 1994 (ITEM 14-A to 14-D). He was last seen entering the
building at 10:52 PM on November 9, and disappeared during that shift. His desk clock had stopped
at 2:00. **Continuity clue:** his last tape is stamped *NOV. 9, 2:21 AM*, which is *before* he
arrived. Amundsen's notes flag it. The camcorder's clock was right on every other tape.
*(writer's eyes only)* Tape 14-D was recorded on the tenth. The date stamp is the floor's date,
not his: on three it's always the night it wants.

**Det. Ruth Amundsen**, Coulee County Sheriff's Department. Caught the Kessler case in 1994 and
never let it go. Over seven years she tracked down every recording connected to the building:
county archives, estate sales, pawn shops, a realtor's drawer. She numbered them **in the order
they were recorded, not the order she found them.** That's why Dale's tapes are ITEM 14. She
retired in 2001. The file was never closed.

**The uploader** *(never identified)*. Somebody with access to the Brenner file started posting
the tapes as **VHS-C 14**. Why, and how they got them, is a mystery for late in the series.

## Motifs and easter eggs (keep these consistent)

- **9**: the official count. Every document says nine.
- **2:11 AM**: Harold's hour. Put it on clocks, timestamps and receipts in every video.
- **The desk clock stopped at 2:00.**
- **Three knocks**, slow and heavy.
- **Wrong date stamps.** On the third floor, clocks and date stamps drift to the floor's night.
- **555-0141**: the dead third-floor extension. It turns up on signs, business cards and phone bills.
- **CAM 05**: a feed that shouldn't exist.
- **Door plaques 310–314** are claimed. **315, 316, 317** are the doors Gary counts at 2:11 AM
  in ITEM 15: the tenants still to come.
- **"Priced to move." "Space available."** The building is always, cheerfully, for rent.
- **Flicker, then movement.** The Occupant is never seen moving, only having moved.

## Mysteries that should never be answered

- What the Occupant is, exactly.
- Whether the tenants are alive.
- Who recorded ITEM 20.
- Who the uploader is and why they're posting the file.
- What happened to the third floor's "space" when the building came down.

## Where the story goes

1. **ITEM 15, SPACE AVAILABLE** (in production): the building advertises itself.
2. **ITEM 17, the emergency broadcast**: the rules go public. It's the pivot from one building
   to *any* building.
3. **ITEM 3, Harold's reels**: the origin, audio only, over photos of the empty new building.
   The scariest episode could have almost no picture.
4. **ITEM 13, CAM 05**: the security installer and a fifth feed. Short, mean, very rewatchable.
5. **ITEM 11, Pruitt's answering machine**: messages from people who are gone, in their own voices.
6. **ITEM 18, the apartment**: the first time it happens somewhere else, and the first time the
   counter is a child.
7. **ITEM 20** is the finale.
